# Channel audit — 26 September 2026

Scope: all 112 film entries, including nine incoming Downloads additions. This is an editorial genre-placement audit, not a full listening review, source-completeness review or rights clearance. The complete retained/moved ledger is in channel-audit.json; full playlists are in stations.md.

## Station definitions

- Repertory: the complete library in an alternating genre mix, each film once per cycle.
- Noir & Mystery: noir, crime stories, detective mysteries and psychological suspense, including earlier gangster films.
- Comedy: screwball, farce, slapstick, satire and comedy of manners. A few songs do not automatically make a film a Musicals selection.
- Drama & Romance: family and social drama, melodrama, romance, biography and historical adventure without a stronger genre home.
- Musicals: musical comedy, revues, band and performance stories, and music-led biographies.
- Sci-Fi & Fantasy: science fiction, space adventure, lost worlds, fantasy, mythology and sword-and-sorcery. These remain one channel.
- Westerns: frontier stories, cavalry adventures, singing westerns and western/industrial hybrids.

## Borderline placements retained

- Tulsa stays in Westerns: it is an oil-boom drama rather than a conventional frontier gunfight story, but [TCM identifies both Drama and Western](https://www.tcm.com/watchtcm/titles/26005). This is an intentional hybrid, not a mistaken date/setting assumption.
- Springtime in the Rockies stays in Westerns as a singing western.
- The Inspector General stays in Comedy: [TCM describes a musical-comedy farce](https://www.tcm.com/articles/92556/the-inspector-general); the mistaken-identity comedy drives its placement. At War with the Army also stays in Comedy ([AFI](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/27103-AT-WAR-WITH-THE-ARMY)).
- Animal Crackers stays in Comedy despite musical numbers; The Animal Kingdom stays there as a comedy of manners ([TCM](https://www.tcm.com/articles/87350/the-animal-kingdom)).
- A Star Is Born is the 1937 dramatic film, not one of the later musical versions; it stays in Drama & Romance.
- Both Scarlet Pimpernel films stay in Drama & Romance as historical adventure. An extra two-film adventure station is not needed.
- Jungle Book (1942), Hercules, Hundra, the Sinbad film and the sword-and-sorcery additions belong in the combined Sci-Fi & Fantasy station. Lost Continent and Unknown World fit its lost-world/scientific-adventure side.
- Thunderbolt (1929) joins Noir & Mystery as an early gangster/crime melodrama; the station is not restricted to the classic noir era.

## Integrity and exclusions

Each movie has exactly one primary genre station and one Repertory entry. No duplicate catalogue entry was added for the Downloads file named DOA (1955): its opening credits identify the existing D.O.A. film. Earth Star Voyager, Indestructible Man and Son of Zorro remain excluded. Previously removed episodic TV and radio series remain absent. No film was placed somewhere merely to reach a numerical channel target.
